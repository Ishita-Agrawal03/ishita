# Institute of Applied Sciences and Technology
## Academic and Administrative Regulations (2025–26 Edition)

**Document control:** This handbook supersedes all previous editions. In case of conflict between this document and a School-level notice, this document prevails unless the notice is issued by the Office of the Registrar with an explicit amendment number.

---

## Section 1: Scope and Applicability

1.1 These regulations apply to all students enrolled in undergraduate (B.Tech, B.Sc) and postgraduate (M.Tech, M.Sc, MBA) programmes of the Institute, across all campuses, from the academic year in which they are admitted, unless superseded by a later edition that is explicitly made retroactive.

1.2 Where a specific School (Engineering, Sciences, Management) has its own supplementary code, that code applies in addition to, not instead of, this document. In the event of direct contradiction between a School code and this handbook on a matter of examination eligibility, this handbook governs.

1.3 "Semester" refers to one of the two regular teaching terms (Odd: July–December, Even: January–May). Summer terms are governed separately under Section 13.

1.4 "Working day" excludes Sundays and Institute-declared holidays but includes Saturdays unless a Saturday is specifically declared a holiday.

---

## Section 2: Academic Calendar and Registration

2.1 Every student must complete course registration within the first two weeks of a semester. Late registration beyond this window requires approval from the Dean of Academic Affairs and attracts a late registration fee as specified in the Fee Schedule (see fee_deadlines.md).

2.2 A student who fails to register for two consecutive semesters without an approved leave of absence is deemed to have withdrawn from the programme.

2.3 Add/drop of courses is permitted only within the first ten working days of a semester. After this period, a course may only be dropped with a "W" (Withdrawn) grade recorded, up to the midpoint of the semester.

---

## Section 3: Attendance Requirement for Examination Eligibility

3.1 Regular attendance in all registered courses, including lectures, tutorials, and laboratory sessions, is mandatory. Attendance is calculated per course, not as an overall average across courses.

3.2 A student must maintain a minimum of **75% attendance** in a course to be eligible to sit for the End-Semester Examination in that course. Attendance is computed from the first day of classes to the last working day before the examination period begins.

3.3 A student who falls below 75% attendance in a course will be awarded an "I" (Ineligible) grade for that course's End-Semester Examination, regardless of internal assessment performance, unless relief is granted under Section 3.4 or Section 7.

3.4 The Examination Eligibility Committee, chaired by the Associate Dean (Academics), may condone attendance shortfall down to a floor of 65% on a case-by-case basis, where the shortfall arose from circumstances the Committee considers beyond the student's control (e.g., participation in Institute-sanctioned sporting or cultural events, participation in placement interviews, or bereavement in the immediate family). Condonation is not automatic and must be applied for in writing within five working days of the attendance shortfall being notified.

3.5 There is no provision under this Section for condonation of attendance shortfall arising from personal travel, family functions, or private commitments not falling under Section 3.4 or the Medical Exemption Clause (Section 7).

3.6 Attendance shortfalls arising from participation in a University-recognised strike or protest action are treated under the Institute's separate Student Conduct Code and are not eligible for condonation under this Section.

---

## Section 4: Internal Assessment and Grading

4.1 Internal assessment (quizzes, assignments, mid-semester examinations) constitutes 40% of the final grade in each course; the End-Semester Examination constitutes the remaining 60%, unless a course syllabus approved by the Board of Studies specifies a different split.

4.2 Grades are awarded on a 10-point scale: O (10), A+ (9), A (8), B+ (7), B (6), C (5), P (4), F (0).

4.3 A student who receives an "I" (Ineligible) grade under Section 3.3 may not appear for that course's End-Semester Examination in the same semester. The course must be repeated in a subsequent semester or during the Summer Term (Section 13).

4.4 Revaluation of an End-Semester Examination answer script may be requested within seven working days of result declaration, on payment of the revaluation fee specified in the Fee Schedule. Revaluation is not permitted for internal assessment components.

4.5 A student who believes a grade was awarded in error due to a clerical mistake (e.g., transcription error, addition error) may request a grade recheck free of charge within three working days of result declaration; this is distinct from revaluation and does not involve re-marking of the answer script.

---

## Section 5: Minimum CGPA and Academic Probation

5.1 A student must maintain a minimum Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA) of 5.0 to remain in good academic standing.

5.2 A student whose CGPA falls below 5.0 at the end of any semester (after the first two semesters of the programme) is placed on Academic Probation.

5.3 A student on Academic Probation for two consecutive semesters is referred to the Academic Standards Committee, which may recommend continuation with conditions, a reduced course load, or discontinuation from the programme.

5.4 Academic Probation status does not, by itself, affect a student's eligibility to sit for examinations; that is governed exclusively by Section 3 (Attendance) and Section 6 (Academic Integrity).

---

## Section 6: Academic Integrity

6.1 Academic dishonesty includes, but is not limited to: plagiarism, unauthorised collaboration, use of unauthorised material during an examination, impersonation, and fabrication of data or results.

6.2 A first proven instance of academic dishonesty in an internal assessment component results in a zero for that component and a formal warning recorded in the student's file.

6.3 A first proven instance of academic dishonesty in an End-Semester Examination results in an F grade for the course and suspension from the Institute for one semester.

6.4 A second proven instance of academic dishonesty, in any component and at any time during the programme, results in expulsion from the Institute. There is no provision for appeal against a second instance beyond the procedural review described in Section 6.5.

6.5 A student may seek procedural review (not a re-hearing of facts) of an Academic Integrity Committee decision within five working days, limited to claims that due process was not followed. Procedural review does not extend to disputing the factual finding of dishonesty itself.

---

## Section 7: Medical Exemption and Leave of Absence

7.1 A student unable to attend classes due to illness for a continuous period exceeding five working days must submit a medical certificate from a registered medical practitioner to the Dean of Student Affairs within ten working days of resuming classes.

7.2 A student who submits a valid medical certificate under 7.1 for an illness lasting between five and fifteen working days will have the affected period excluded from the attendance denominator when computing eligibility under Section 3.2, effectively raising their achievable attendance percentage for the remainder of the semester.

7.3 A student hospitalised or medically incapacitated for a period exceeding fifteen working days in a semester may apply for a Medical Leave of Absence for that semester, in which case no grades are recorded and the semester does not count toward the maximum duration of the programme.

7.4 Nothing in this Section overrides the 75% attendance floor in Section 3.2; the Medical Exemption Clause adjusts the denominator used to compute attendance percentage, it does not lower the 75% threshold itself. A student must still achieve 75% of the adjusted total to sit the End-Semester Examination.

7.5 Leave of absence for reasons other than medical incapacitation (including family events, personal travel, or non-Institute-sanctioned competitions) is governed by Section 3.5 and does not qualify for attendance denominator adjustment.

## Section 8: Scholarships and Financial Aid

8.1 The Institute offers Merit Scholarships (based on academic performance), Need-Based Scholarships (based on documented family income), and Sports Scholarships (based on state or national-level sporting achievement).

8.2 Merit Scholarship recipients must maintain a CGPA of at least 8.0 and satisfactory conduct to continue receiving the scholarship in subsequent semesters. A recipient whose CGPA falls below 8.0 in any semester loses the scholarship from the following semester but may reapply if CGPA recovers.

8.3 Need-Based Scholarship recipients must maintain a CGPA of at least 6.0 and submit updated family income documentation annually.

8.4 Sports Scholarship recipients must maintain a CGPA of at least 5.0 and continue to represent the Institute in their sport as required by the Department of Sports.

8.5 All scholarship categories require the recipient to be in good academic standing with respect to attendance and examination eligibility as defined in Sections 3 and 6; a student rendered ineligible to sit an examination under Section 3.3 in a given semester is not automatically disqualified from a scholarship for that reason alone, provided the ineligibility does not also trigger loss of standing under Section 5.

---

## Section 9: Fee Payment

9.1 Tuition and hostel fees must be paid according to the schedule published each semester (see fee_deadlines.md for the current academic year).

9.2 A student who fails to pay fees by the due date is charged a late fee. The late fee is a **flat charge of ₹500 per week or part thereof** of delay, applied uniformly regardless of the total fee amount outstanding, up to a maximum of four weeks, after which the student's registration is provisionally cancelled pending payment.

9.3 A student whose registration is provisionally cancelled under 9.2 is not permitted to attend classes or sit examinations until fees are paid in full, including accumulated late fees. Attendance during the period of provisional cancellation does not count toward the Section 3.2 requirement.

9.4 Fee waivers or deferred payment plans may be granted by the Finance Committee on documented financial hardship grounds, on written application at least fifteen working days before the due date.

---

## Section 10: Examination Conduct

10.1 Students must carry their Institute identity card to every examination. A student without an identity card may be permitted to write the examination against a provisional slip issued by the invigilator, subject to verification.

10.2 Electronic devices, including smartwatches and phones, are prohibited in examination halls unless a specific course syllabus permits calculators or specified software, in which case only Institute-issued or pre-approved devices may be used.

10.3 A student arriving more than 30 minutes after the start of an examination will not be permitted to enter the examination hall. No extra time is granted for late arrival within the permitted window.

10.4 A student who needs to leave the examination hall temporarily (e.g., for medical reasons) must be escorted by an invigilator and the time taken is not compensated unless the Chief Superintendent certifies an exceptional circumstance.

---

## Section 11: Grievance Redressal

11.1 Any student grievance relating to academic matters (grading disputes beyond Section 4.4/4.5, harassment by faculty, discrimination) should first be raised with the Head of Department informally.

11.2 If unresolved within ten working days, the grievance may be escalated in writing to the Grievance Redressal Committee (GRC), which must convene within fifteen working days of receiving the written complaint.

11.3 The GRC's recommendations are advisory to the Dean of Academic Affairs, who issues the final decision within ten working days of receiving the GRC's report.

11.4 Grievances relating to sexual harassment are governed exclusively by the Institute's Internal Complaints Committee (ICC) process under the Prevention of Sexual Harassment framework, not by this Section.

---

## Section 12: Committee Powers and Waivers

12.1 The Examination Eligibility Committee (Section 3.4), the Academic Standards Committee (Section 5.3), and the Academic Integrity Committee (Section 6) each operate within the specific powers granted to them in their respective sections and may not expand their own jurisdiction.

12.2 Only the Registrar, acting on the recommendation of the Dean of Academic Affairs, may grant a waiver of a regulation not otherwise covered by a specific committee's powers, and only in circumstances the Registrar records in writing as "wholly exceptional."

12.3 Scholarship Continuation Review: the Scholarship Committee reviews continuation eligibility at the end of each semester, applying the CGPA thresholds in Section 8 together with the attendance requirement of **80% as specified in Section 3**, to determine whether a recipient remains in good standing for the following semester.

12.4 Decisions of the Registrar under 12.2 are final and not subject to further appeal within the Institute.

---

## Section 13: Summer Term

13.1 The Summer Term is an optional, shortened term offered between the Even and Odd semesters, primarily for students repeating a failed or ineligible course.

13.2 Attendance requirements in the Summer Term follow Section 3 in full; there is no relaxation of the 75% threshold for Summer Term courses.

13.3 A maximum of two courses may be registered in a Summer Term.

13.4 Summer Term fees are charged per course and are not covered by any scholarship category under Section 8.

---

## Section 14: Degree Requirements and Duration

14.1 The standard duration of the B.Tech programme is eight semesters; the maximum permitted duration, including any Medical Leave of Absence under Section 7.3, is fourteen semesters.

14.2 A student must clear all courses in the prescribed curriculum, meet the minimum CGPA requirement of Section 5.1, and have no pending disciplinary action under Section 6 to be eligible for the degree to be conferred.

14.3 Students completing all requirements ahead of the standard duration may graduate early with approval from the Dean of Academic Affairs, provided minimum residency of six semesters has been completed.

---

## Section 15: Hostel and Residential Matters

15.1 On-campus hostel accommodation is governed primarily by the Hostel Handbook (separate document, hostel_policy.pdf), which is incorporated by reference into these regulations for matters of student conduct and residential discipline.

15.2 Where the Hostel Handbook is silent on a matter of academic consequence (e.g., whether a hostel disciplinary action affects examination eligibility), this main regulations document governs: hostel disciplinary action alone does not affect examination eligibility unless it also constitutes a proven instance of academic dishonesty under Section 6.

---

## Section 16: Internships and Placements

16.1 Final-year students may undertake a credit-bearing internship in their final semester, subject to prior approval from the Department's Internship Coordinator and a minimum CGPA of 6.0 at the time of application.

16.2 An internship undertaken during a regular semester (not the final semester internship track) does not exempt a student from the attendance requirement of Section 3.2 for courses registered in that semester, except where the Department has pre-approved specific classes as "internship-adjustable" at the start of the semester.

16.3 Students attending a placement interview or placement-related test organised through the Institute's Placement Cell are treated under Section 3.4 as an Institute-sanctioned activity eligible for attendance condonation, provided the Placement Cell certifies the student's participation.

16.4 A student who accepts a placement offer through the Institute's Placement Cell and subsequently withdraws from the process without valid reason may be barred from further placement opportunities for that academic year, at the discretion of the Placement Cell, subject to review by the Dean of Student Affairs.

16.5 Placement and internship matters not covered in this Section are governed by the Placement Policy document maintained separately by the Placement Cell, which is not part of these Regulations.

## Section 17: Anti-Ragging and Student Conduct

17.1 The Institute maintains a zero-tolerance Anti-Ragging Policy, administered by the Anti-Ragging Committee, which operates independently of the academic and hostel disciplinary processes described elsewhere in this document.

17.2 A student found guilty of ragging, in any form and irrespective of severity, is subject to a minimum penalty of one-semester suspension, in addition to any hostel-level or academic consequence arising from the same incident under Sections 6 or 8 of the Hostel Handbook.

17.3 Reports of ragging may be made anonymously through the Institute's helpline, and the identity of a complainant, if disclosed, is protected to the extent possible under applicable law.

17.4 General student conduct expectations outside the specific contexts of examinations (Section 10), hostels (Hostel Handbook), and ragging (17.1-17.3) are set out in the Institute's Student Conduct Code, a separate document available from the Dean of Student Affairs' office, which also governs conduct during Institute-organised off-campus trips and industrial visits.

## Appendix A: Definitions

- **Condonation**: A discretionary decision by an authorised committee to overlook a shortfall against a stated requirement, without changing the requirement itself.
- **Ineligible ("I") grade**: A grade indicating a student was barred from an End-Semester Examination due to attendance shortfall, distinct from an "F" (Fail) grade which reflects examination performance.
- **Good academic standing**: A composite status requiring CGPA ≥ 5.0, no active disciplinary suspension, and no more than one semester of consecutive Academic Probation.
- **Provisional cancellation**: Temporary suspension of a student's registration due to non-payment of fees, reversible on payment in full.

## Appendix B: Cross-Reference Notes for Administrative Staff

- Attendance eligibility (Section 3) and Medical Exemption (Section 7) interact only through the denominator adjustment described in 7.2; they do not create two different percentage thresholds. Any document quoting a threshold other than 75% (or the 65% condonation floor in 3.4) for examination eligibility should be flagged for correction.
- The Scholarship Committee's practice under Section 12.3 should, pending correction, be read as referring to the Section 3.2 threshold, whatever number is used there; staff fielding student queries on this point should escalate to the Registrar's office rather than quote either figure as authoritative.
- Late fee provisions appear in two places (Section 9.2 of this document and the Fee Schedule's Late Payment Policy); until reconciled, Accounts Office staff should apply the calculation method specified in the current year's Fee Schedule circular, and flag the discrepancy rather than resolve it unilaterally.
- Hostel curfew timing is set out in the Hostel Handbook, not this document; queries about curfew should be directed to the Hostel Office, not answered from this Regulations document.
