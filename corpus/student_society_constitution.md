# Constitution of the Institute Student Council and Affiliated Societies

## Article 1: Name and Purpose

1.1 This document constitutes the governing framework for the Institute Student Council ("the Council") and all Institute-recognised student societies, clubs, and chapters (collectively, "Societies").

1.2 The Council exists to represent student interests to the Institute administration, coordinate inter-society activities, and allocate the Student Activity Fund.

1.3 A Society is any group of at least fifteen enrolled students formally recognised by the Council under Article 4, operating in a technical, cultural, literary, or service-oriented domain.

## Article 2: Council Composition

2.1 The Council consists of a General Secretary, a Cultural Secretary, a Technical Secretary, a Sports Secretary, and one Class Representative per academic year per programme.

2.2 Elections for the General Secretary, Cultural Secretary, Technical Secretary, and Sports Secretary are held annually in the first four weeks of the Odd Semester, open to all students who have completed at least two semesters at the Institute and are in good academic standing as defined in the main Regulations.

2.3 A student on Academic Probation (as defined in Section 5 of the Academic and Administrative Regulations) at the time of nomination is not eligible to contest a Council office, though they remain eligible to vote.

2.4 Class Representatives are elected by their respective class within the first two weeks of each Odd Semester and serve a one-year term.

2.5 The Council's faculty advisor, appointed annually by the Dean of Student Affairs, has an advisory but non-voting role in all Council decisions.

## Article 3: Term and Removal

3.1 Council office-bearers serve a term of one academic year, from the date of election result declaration to the declaration of the following year's results.

3.2 An office-bearer may be removed before the end of their term by a two-thirds majority vote of the Council, on grounds of gross misconduct, prolonged absence from Council duties (more than four consecutive meetings without valid reason), or loss of good academic standing.

3.3 A vacancy arising from removal, resignation, or a student's withdrawal from the Institute is filled by a by-election held within three weeks of the vacancy arising, following the same eligibility criteria as Article 2.

## Article 4: Recognition of Societies

4.1 A new Society seeking recognition must submit a written constitution, a list of at least fifteen founding members, and a statement of purpose to the Council's Technical Secretary or Cultural Secretary, as appropriate to its domain.

4.2 The Council reviews new Society applications quarterly and grants or denies recognition by simple majority vote. A denied application may be resubmitted after addressing the Council's stated concerns.

4.3 A recognised Society must hold at least two events or activities per semester and submit a brief activity report to the Council at the end of each semester to retain recognition.

4.4 A Society that fails to meet the requirement in 4.3 for two consecutive semesters, without an approved reason, has its recognition automatically suspended and must reapply for recognition under Article 4.1.

4.5 The Council may revoke a Society's recognition at any time, by two-thirds majority vote, for conduct that violates the Institute's Student Conduct Code or brings the Institute into disrepute.

## Article 5: Student Activity Fund

5.1 The Student Activity Fund is financed through a mandatory per-semester contribution collected alongside tuition fees, at a rate set annually by the Council and approved by the Dean of Student Affairs.

5.2 Funds are allocated to recognised Societies on a quarterly basis, based on written proposals submitted to the Council's Treasurer (an appointed, non-elected position reporting to the General Secretary).

5.3 A Society requesting funds exceeding ₹50,000 for a single event must additionally obtain approval from the Dean of Student Affairs before funds are disbursed.

5.4 Unspent funds allocated to a Society in a given quarter do not carry forward automatically; a Society wishing to retain unspent funds for a future event must submit a specific written request to the Treasurer before the end of the quarter.

5.5 All Society expenditures must be supported by original receipts submitted to the Treasurer within two weeks of the expenditure; failure to do so may result in the amount being deducted from the Society's future allocations.

## Article 6: Events and External Sponsorship

6.1 A Society or the Council wishing to hold an event involving external participants (from other institutions or the public) must seek approval from the Dean of Student Affairs at least three weeks in advance, stating expected attendance, venue, and safety arrangements.

6.2 External sponsorship for a Society event must be disclosed to the Council's Treasurer and approved by the Dean of Student Affairs before any sponsorship agreement is finalised, particularly where the sponsor is a for-profit entity.

6.3 No Society event may serve alcohol or permit smoking on campus, consistent with the Institute's general prohibition under the Hostel Handbook and Student Conduct Code.

6.4 A Society event extending past 9:00 PM on a weekday requires prior written approval from the Dean of Student Affairs, specifying arrangements for student safety and transport, independent of the general hostel curfew provisions.

## Article 7: Inter-Society Disputes and Grievances

7.1 Disputes between Societies (e.g., over shared venue bookings, overlapping event dates, or fund allocation disagreements) are first brought to the Council's General Secretary for informal mediation.

7.2 If unresolved within two weeks, a dispute may be referred to the full Council for a vote, which is binding on both Societies.

7.3 A grievance against the Council itself (e.g., alleged unfair fund allocation, denial of recognition believed to be arbitrary) may be escalated to the Dean of Student Affairs, whose decision is final with respect to Council-level matters, without prejudice to any separate grievance a student may raise under Section 11 of the main Regulations for matters that also constitute an academic grievance.

## Article 8: Amendments

8.1 This Constitution may be amended by a two-thirds majority vote of the Council, followed by ratification from the Dean of Student Affairs.

8.2 Proposed amendments must be circulated to all recognised Societies at least two weeks before the vote.

8.3 This Constitution does not govern matters of academic standing, examination eligibility, hostel residency, or fee payment, which remain exclusively governed by the Academic and Administrative Regulations and the Hostel Handbook respectively. Where a Society activity intersects with those matters (for example, a student missing a mandatory event due to an academic clash), the Academic and Administrative Regulations take precedence.

## Article 9: Dissolution

9.1 The Council may be dissolved and reconstituted only by a directive of the Institute's Board of Governors; no provision in this Constitution permits the Council to dissolve itself.

9.2 A Society may voluntarily dissolve by submitting a written notice signed by a majority of its current members to the Council, at which point any remaining allocated funds revert to the Student Activity Fund.
